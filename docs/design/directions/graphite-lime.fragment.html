Direction B — Graphite & Lime

Neutral graphite with no colour tint, so nothing competes with the data. One electric lime accent, used sparingly for the primary action, the active menu item and the moment an import lands. Vivid per-metric colours pop on the grey base: still a calm, roomy instrument panel, with a bit more energy than a navy or teal direction.

Inspired by Apple Fitness (vivid metric colours on true-dark), Whoop (graphite surfaces, a single bright signal colour) and the lime of a cycling jersey seen at dusk.

No metric is lime or yellow-green, so nothing collides with the accent. All metric colours pass 4.5:1 on surface in both themes, so they work as text, not only as graphics. Colour-vision safety: in dark mode the metrics sit in two lightness tiers (light: speed, moving time, power; mid: distance, heart rate, elevation). Within a tier the hues are split along the blue–yellow axis, which red-green deficiencies keep (cyan vs amber vs silver; blue vs pink vs violet). Moving time is a neutral silver/grey, readable in any vision type. Colour is never the only cue: every value keeps its label and its fixed position in the metric order.

06Why pick this / trade-offs

  • Most legible data. A colour-free grey base means six metric colours can be vivid without clashing with a tinted background; closest to the Apple Fitness feel.
  • Clear single signal. Lime only marks the primary action, the active menu item and "Feature"/new, so it always means "act here" or "this is new".
  • Light mode needs two lime tokens. Lime fails as text on light backgrounds (1.2:1), so light mode uses lime only as fill and #4D6B00 for text. That is one more rule to follow in code.
  • More energetic, slightly less calm. Six saturated colours plus lime is the busiest of the directions; it relies on generous whitespace and muted labels to stay calm.
  • Neutral can feel generic. Without a tinted surface, the identity rests on the lime and the wordmark dot rather than on an overall colour mood.
