Design direction · Cycling Hub

Direction C — Deep Ocean

The quietest of the three. Blue-tinted slate surfaces that fade very slightly from top to bottom, cards that float on soft layered shadows instead of borders, and one cobalt accent that only marks what you can act on. The numbers stay the loudest thing on the screen, in muted, sea-washed metric colours.

Inspired by Oura's soft depth, Apple Health's calm data tables and the colour of the North Sea at dusk. Font: Inter, one family everywhere.

No metric is blue: the azure/cobalt accent owns blue, speed is a pale ice (dark) or steel cyan (light) with a big lightness gap to the accent. Colour-vision safety comes from lightness steps, not hue alone: in dark mode the set spans L* 56 (rose) to 93 (ivory), and the classic red-green pair, distance vs heart rate, is 21 L* apart (sea green 77, rose 56). Simulated deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia keep every pair at ΔE ≥ 13. Metric values always carry their unit and a label, so colour is never the only cue. All are ≥ 4.1 : 1 on surface, so they also pass as large text.

Why pick this / trade-offs

Why pick this
  • The calmest option: depth comes from soft shadows and a faint gradient, not from colour, so long sessions with the ride table stay restful.
  • One accent with one job (cobalt = tappable), which keeps the six metric colours readable as data, not decoration.
  • Light mode is designed, not inverted: blue-grey whites, darker metric tones, the same soft elevation.
Trade-offs
  • Muted metric colours have less punch than Apple Fitness rings; charts later will need line weight and fills to carry them.
  • Soft shadows almost disappear on very dark or low-quality screens; card edges then rely on the subtle surface step.
  • Least distinctive of the three: elegant, but with less brand personality.
