Cycling Hub — design directions V1

Three high-level directions built on your design brief (calm & analytical, dark-first with an equally good light mode, blue/teal or green/lime accents, one clean sans, soft rounded shapes, fixed colour per metric). They differ in surface tone, accent and how vivid the metric colours are. Pick one, or combine parts. Numbers are sample data. After you choose, the wireframes become real designs plus DESIGN.md and design tokens.

Direction A

Direction A — Midnight Teal

A quiet instrument panel at night. Deep navy-ink surfaces (blue-black, never pure black) let the numbers stand out, and one teal accent marks the thing that matters most on each screen. Calm and moody in the dark; crisp cool paper with ink text in the light.

Inspired by Whoop and Oura night screens, with the metric-colour discipline of Apple Fitness. Font: Inter.

Why pick this / trade-offs

Why pick it

  • The closest match to the "quiet instrument panel": navy-ink is softer on the eyes than pure black, and the six metric colours read like gauges on it.
  • One accent per screen: it is always clear where to tap or look first, without decoration.
  • Every colour, metrics included, passes 4.5 : 1 in both themes, so the same tokens work for text, charts and future zones.

Trade-offs

  • Moody and a bit "night-time": less energetic than a lime-led direction, and the teal sits close to many health apps.
  • Six saturated metric colours on dark navy can feel busy in dense tables; on desktop we may need to tone them down to the dot only.
  • Without glow, the dark surfaces rely on small lightness steps between layers; they need care on low-quality screens.

Direction B — Graphite & Lime

Neutral graphite with no colour tint, so nothing competes with the data. One electric lime accent, used sparingly for the primary action, the active menu item and the moment an import lands. Vivid per-metric colours pop on the grey base: still a calm, roomy instrument panel, with a bit more energy than a navy or teal direction.

Inspired by Apple Fitness (vivid metric colours on true-dark), Whoop (graphite surfaces, a single bright signal colour) and the lime of a cycling jersey seen at dusk.

06Why pick this / trade-offs

  • Most legible data. A colour-free grey base means six metric colours can be vivid without clashing with a tinted background; closest to the Apple Fitness feel.
  • Clear single signal. Lime only marks the primary action, the active menu item and "Feature"/new, so it always means "act here" or "this is new".
  • Light mode needs two lime tokens. Lime fails as text on light backgrounds (1.2:1), so light mode uses lime only as fill and #4D6B00 for text. That is one more rule to follow in code.
  • More energetic, slightly less calm. Six saturated colours plus lime is the busiest of the directions; it relies on generous whitespace and muted labels to stay calm.
  • Neutral can feel generic. Without a tinted surface, the identity rests on the lime and the wordmark dot rather than on an overall colour mood.

Design direction · Cycling Hub

Direction C — Deep Ocean

The quietest of the three. Blue-tinted slate surfaces that fade very slightly from top to bottom, cards that float on soft layered shadows instead of borders, and one cobalt accent that only marks what you can act on. The numbers stay the loudest thing on the screen, in muted, sea-washed metric colours.

Inspired by Oura's soft depth, Apple Health's calm data tables and the colour of the North Sea at dusk. Font: Inter, one family everywhere.

Why pick this / trade-offs

Why pick this
  • The calmest option: depth comes from soft shadows and a faint gradient, not from colour, so long sessions with the ride table stay restful.
  • One accent with one job (cobalt = tappable), which keeps the six metric colours readable as data, not decoration.
  • Light mode is designed, not inverted: blue-grey whites, darker metric tones, the same soft elevation.
Trade-offs
  • Muted metric colours have less punch than Apple Fitness rings; charts later will need line weight and fills to carry them.
  • Soft shadows almost disappear on very dark or low-quality screens; card edges then rely on the subtle surface step.
  • Least distinctive of the three: elegant, but with less brand personality.
